Glitch Lab Facilitator Guide

Everything you need to run the AI escape room with student teams: how to get in, what each puzzle asks, how to help when a team is stuck, and how to wrap up. No technical knowledge needed.

The game (for students) glitchapp.vercel.app

Open this on every team's device.

Start a fresh game glitchapp.vercel.app/new

Wipes that device and goes to the title screen. Use between groups.

Facilitator console glitchapp.vercel.app/dev

Lets you mark puzzles solved. For adults only. See "When something goes wrong".

How the game works

Students are locked in an AI research lab with Glitch, a lab robot whose AI got scrambled. They click glowing objects in the lab to open puzzles. Each puzzle teaches one idea about how AI works.

  • Four puzzles are open from the start, in any order: Feedback Maze, Bad Data, Prompt Precision and Fact or Fiction.
  • Three of them give the team a colored digit. Fact or Fiction gives a key instead.
  • When all four are solved, the keypad by the door switches on. Teams enter their three digits in the color order printed on the handout to escape.
  • The end screen shows the team's total time and a short list of four habits for using AI.
Every team has the same door code. It's in the answer key at the back. Keep those pages away from students, and keep an ear out for teams calling the code across the room.

Before the session

  1. Check the devices. Open glitchapp.vercel.app on each team's laptop or tablet. Chrome is the most tested browser. If you plan to use iPads or another browser, play a puzzle on one first.

  2. Use a normal browser window, not a private or incognito one. The game saves progress in the browser. A private window loses everything when it's closed.

  3. Reset every device right before students arrive. Open glitchapp.vercel.app/new on each one. Bookmarking that page makes this one click.

  4. Print the team handouts. Make sure they show the color order for the door code: Green, then Blue, then Red. Teams can't escape without it.

  5. Keep this guide handy, with the answer key at the back kept away from students.

Running the session

  1. Title screen. Students click the START button painted on the picture. Glitch tells a short story in four screens. They can read it or press Skip intro.

  2. The lab. Glowing blue boxes mark the puzzles. Hovering over one shows its name. Grey boxes are solved; they can still be clicked to play again. The keypad by the door shows a padlock until it's ready.

  3. Puzzles. Every puzzle has a Hint button at the top right. It gives three hints, each more specific than the last. Encourage teams to try one hint before asking you.

  4. Progress check. The Mission Log button in the lab lists what each team has solved, the digits they've earned, and what's still locked.

  5. Escape. After the right code, the door opens and the end screen shows the team's time. The timer starts when they open their first puzzle, not at the title screen.

The puzzles

What students do, what success looks like, and where teams usually get stuck. Full answers are in the answer key at the back.

Feedback Maze

In the lab: the grid panel on the right-hand wall
Students
Help Glitch get from the Lab to the Bridge (marked 🏁 Goal on the map). At each turn Glitch suggests a move and a yellow circle shows where it wants to go. Students press 👍 Good move or 👎 Bad move.
Teaches
Reinforcement learning: AI improves from feedback, and wrong feedback teaches it the wrong thing.
Reward
A Blue digit
Good to know: Glitch's very first suggestion is always a dead end, on purpose. A thumbs-down there is the right call. Each answer gets a short ✓ or ✗ note explaining why. Teams can't get permanently stuck: if they approve a dead end, Glitch backs out and tries again. Start Over sends Glitch back to the Lab but keeps what it has learned.

Bad Data

In the lab: the papers on the front-left table
Students
Read Glitch's silly report about a comet, then mark each of 7 sources as Credible or Not Credible. If your handouts include printouts of the sources, this is where they're used.
Teaches
AI is only as good as its sources. Even with good sources, check the answer yourself.
Reward
A Red digit
Good to know: Check Results stays grey until all 7 are answered; the line under it counts how many are left. A wrong check doesn't say how many are wrong, only that some are, and the answers stay so students can change them. Afterwards, Glitch shows a corrected report that lists its sources and links, followed by a reminder to check AI answers yourself. A good question to ask: would you trust this report without opening any of its links?

Prompt Precision

In the lab: the computer screen on the right-hand desk
Students
Build a prompt for the ship's computer by filling 6 slots: Persona, Context, Task, Constraints, Tone / Style and Output Format. Each slot has 5 cards to choose from.
Teaches
Clear, specific prompts get better results, and a person checks AI's work before acting on anything important.
Reward
A Green digit
Good to know: Click a slot's ▸ Cards line to see its options. Tap a card, then tap the slot; dragging also works on a computer. A card can only go in its own slot. When a check fails, Glitch complains about one problem at a time, so the team swaps one card and checks again. This is by design, and the cards stay in place.

Fact or Fiction

In the lab: the open book on the middle desk
Students
Sort 8 statements about AI into Fact or Fiction. Tap a statement, then tap Place here on a column (or drag it).
Teaches
What AI can and can't do: it can sound confident and be wrong, invent sources, and pick up bias from its data.
Reward
A key (no digit). This is the 4th puzzle needed to switch on the keypad.
Good to know: Exactly four are facts and four are fiction. A wrong check just says some are in the wrong column; students move cards and check again. After solving, each fiction statement shows a corrected version, which makes a good discussion prompt.

Final Console (the keypad)

In the lab: the small panel beside the door on the left
Students
Enter their three colored digits in the order on the handout.
Unlocks
Only after all four other puzzles are solved.
Green firstBlue secondRed third
Good to know: The keypad lists the clues in puzzle order (Blue, Red, Green), not in code order, so students need the handout. After 3 wrong codes the keypad cools down for 30 seconds. Watch out: each puzzle's screen color is not its clue color. The maze screen is purple but its digit is Blue, and Prompt Precision's screen is blue but its digit is Green. Point students to the colored circle on the reward screen.

When something goes wrong

What you seeWhat to do
The keypad has a padlock or says it's still lockedAll four other puzzles must be solved first. Open the Mission Log from the lab to see which one is missing.
"Keypad cooling down… 30s"The team entered 3 wrong codes. Wait 30 seconds. Meanwhile, check the handout's color order and the digits shown on the keypad.
The code doesn't workUsually the digits are in the wrong order. It's Green, then Blue, then Red. Hovering over a solved object in the lab shows the digit it gave. The full code is in the answer key.
A team's progress disappearedProgress lives in that one browser on that one device. It's lost if they switched devices or browsers, used a private window, or the game was more than 12 hours old. You can give back solved puzzles from /dev on that device (see below).
A new group sits down at a used deviceOpen glitchapp.vercel.app/new. Students can't reset a game mid-way on purpose, so one student can't wipe the team's work.
The lab picture is cut off or too bigZoom the browser out: Ctrl and − (Windows/Chromebook) or ⌘ and − (Mac).
Cards won't drag on a tabletTap instead: tap a card, then tap the slot (Prompt Precision) or Place here (Fact or Fiction).
The screen looks old or odd after an updateRefresh the page. Progress is kept.
A team is stuck on a puzzlePoint them to the Hint button first; the third hint is very direct. Then use the answer key to give a nudge, not the answer.
A team runs out of timeOn their device, open /dev and press mark solved next to the puzzle they're on. They can then finish the keypad.
A student wants to replay a puzzleClick its grey box in the lab, or use Play again in the Mission Log. Replaying doesn't change their digits.
About the facilitator console (/dev). It shows the door code and has buttons that change the game: mark solved, Unlock all and Reset all progress. Only open it on a student device when you mean to use it, and close the tab afterwards. Don't switch on its floating toolbar on student devices; that adds "Mark as Solved" buttons to every puzzle.

Wrapping up

The end screen lists four habits. They make a good closing discussion:

  • Check it. AI can sound confident and still be wrong, or invent sources. Ask: in which puzzle did Glitch sound sure but get it wrong?
  • Know where it came from. AI is only as good as the data behind it. Ask: which sources did you trust in Bad Data, and why?
  • Be specific, then refine. Clear prompts get better results, and you can follow up. Ask: what made the winning prompt better than the others?
  • You decide. AI is a tool and people stay in charge. Ask: in the maze, what happened when the feedback was wrong?

To play again, a team presses Play Again on the end screen and confirms. That starts a brand-new game with a fresh timer.

Answer key

For facilitators only. Please don't show these pages to students.

Door code

2 4 6

Green 2 from Prompt Precision, then Blue 4 from Feedback Maze, then Red 6 from Bad Data. The code is the same for every team.

Feedback Maze

Shortest route to the Bridge:

Lab → Medical → Crew Quarters → Rec Room → Gym → Cafe → Bridge

Give 👍 to any move along this route and 👎 to anything else, including Glitch's first suggestion (up, into a dead end). Some short stretches have only one way to go, so Glitch moves on without asking.

Prompt Precision

PersonaCalm, logical starship systems engineer focused on safety and accuracy.
ContextLocked out of the life-support console, oxygen dropping fast.
TaskDraft the repair code to seal the leak in conduit B-7.
ConstraintsUnder 100 words; only commands from the ship's repair manual; no jokes or guesses.
Tone / StyleClear, confident and step-by-step, like a repair manual.
Output FormatStrict SYSTEM OVERRIDE command syntax, ending with a status line.

Glitch reports one wrong slot per check, in this order: Task, Output Format, Constraints, Persona, Context, Tone / Style.

Fact or Fiction

  • FactAI learns by finding patterns in large amounts of data.
  • FictionIf an AI sounds confident, its answer is probably right.
  • FactAI can pick up unfair patterns (bias) from the data it was trained on.
  • FictionIf an AI chatbot gives you a link or a source, you can trust that it exists.
  • FactAI can be trained by giving it rewards for good choices and penalties for bad ones.
  • FictionGetting the same answer from an AI twice proves the answer is correct.
  • FactAI can generate text, images, and even music.
  • FictionA chatbot automatically knows about events that happened after it was trained.

Statements appear in this order in the game.
